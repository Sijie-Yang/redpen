Do Bike Lanes Sell More Coffee?

A 12-week look at four streets and eleven cafés

Demo deck for Redpen · the numbers are made up

Open with the question. Say right away that this is a small study, not a causal claim.

Why we asked

  • Shop owners often oppose new bike lanes because they expect to lose customers who arrive by car.
  • Cafés recieve many short visits, so they should react quickly to a change in foot and bike traffic.
  • We wanted to know whether the cafés on streets that got a protected bike lane in the spring sold more or fewer drinks afterwards than they had sold before, compared with similar cafés on nearby streets that did not get a lane, while also keeping in mind the weather, the school holidays and one street festival.
Spend little time here. The third point is the research question; read it slowly.

What we measured

  • Daily drink sales from the cafés' own tills
  • Six weeks before and six weeks after the lanes opened
  • Six cafés on streets with a new lane, five on streets without

Give the two numbers, then pause. Mention that summer started during the study, which is why both rows go up.

Takeaways

  • In this small sample, cafés next to a new bike lane sold about a quarter more than comparable cafés.
  • Eleven cafés and one season: treat it as a hint, not as proof.
  • Next: repeat in autumn, and add shops that sell things you cannot carry on a bike.
End on the limits. Invite questions about the comparison streets.
